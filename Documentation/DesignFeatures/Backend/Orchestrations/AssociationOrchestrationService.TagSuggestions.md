# Association orchestration service — tag suggestions
Parent: [TagSuggestions.md](../../TagSuggestions.md)
Level: orchestration — `IAssociationOrchestrationService` (`Glory2Him.Core/Services/Orchestrations/Associations/`), existing. Its other user stories are `Backend/Orchestrations/AssociationOrchestrationService.md`, the Likes feature's, and `AssociationOrchestrationService.RejectedEndpoints.md`.
Inherits: §ARC12.3.1 rules 5 and 5a, §ARC12.5 (*an orchestration holds brokers*, and the two-to-three rule), §ARC12.5.2 rule 6, §ARC16.2.1, §ARC16.8.1, §DOM4.4 rule 4, §DOM4.5, §DOM4.6, §SEC14.5 rules 1–3, 6 and 7, §SEC14.6, §SEC14.7 posture A rule 4 and posture A′ rule 1, `Backend/Orchestrations/AssociationOrchestrationService.md` §1 rules 1–3 and §6, `AssociationOrchestrationService.RejectedEndpoints.md §1` rules 2 and 3 and §2 rule 5, `Backend/Foundations/TagService.TagSuggestions.md` §1 and §3, RejectedEndpoints.md rule 12, TagSuggestions.md rules 1–15

The suggestion of a tag for a post by its name (TagSuggestions.md rule 1): the one member this feature adds. It finds the tag the name names or adds it, and pairs it with the post unless the pairing exists, reaching the rules that decide a pairing where this service already holds them. It is a third way in beside the upsert and the `Association-Adding` door, and is neither of them. The write-flow seam's two halves, which hold those two doors to one flow (`AssociationOrchestrationServiceTests.Substrate.WriteFlow.cs`), are unchanged by it; the refusals that seam lists are driven through this member as well, by a theory of its own (§1 rule 8).

## 1. SuggestTagAsync (#1368)

```csharp
ValueTask<AssociationSubmissionResult> SuggestTagAsync(
    TagSuggestion tagSuggestion,
    CancellationToken cancellationToken = default);
```

A new member, direct only (TagSuggestions.md rule 12). Its models are new, under `Glory2Him.Core/Models/Orchestrations/Associations/`, and public, as the upsert's are, because `AssociationsController` binds them:

- **`TagSuggestion`** — the request: `ContentItemId`, a `Guid`, and `Name`, a `string`, and nothing else.
- **`AssociationSubmissionResult`** — the answer: `Status`, an `AssociationSubmissionStatus`, and nothing else (TagSuggestions.md rules 5 and 7). No id.
- **`AssociationSubmissionStatus`** — `Created`, `AlreadySuggested`, `AlreadySuggestedByCaller` and `PreviouslySuggestedByCaller`, in that order. The numbers cross the wire, so a member is appended and none moves, as `AssociationSuggestionStatus`'s summary has it.

**Steps 9 and 10 leave this member** (TagSuggestions.md, *Splitting the suggestion*). The find-or-add, with step 9's withholding and the entry it logs, becomes an operation of its own beneath this one, with a task of its own. Where it sits is TagSuggestions.md open question 6, and both places weighed for it are reopened by the ruling (*Deviations* 1, *What was rejected*). Until it is ruled, the two steps are written here as they stand, and #1368 is not signed off.

1. **The flow, in this order** (TagSuggestions.md rule 9). Each step refuses before the next is asked, and nothing is read or written past a refusal.
   1. **The request.** A `null` suggestion is refused, `NullAssociationOrchestrationException`, *"Tag suggestion is null."* The member then mints its envelope over the suggestion (`IEventEnvelopeBroker.CreateAsync`), as the upsert mints one over its working copy, and every gate below reads the security context from it, never from an ambient accessor (§ARC12.5.2 rule 6, *a read whose answer depends on who is asking is passed the envelope it is being made under*).
   2. **The contribution gate of an editorial pair**: authentication, and the global `ReadOnly` (§SEC14.7 posture A′ rule 1), the two leaves the upsert's composition asks of its editorial pair, `ValidateUserIsAuthenticated` and `ValidateUserIsNotGloballyBlocked` (`AssociationOrchestrationService.Validations.cs`). They are composed for this member in a composition of its own, as every operation's gate is there (*One composition per operation, over shared leaves*), and never through the upsert's `ValidateUserIsAllowedToContribute`, so a rule either operation's gate gains later lands on that operation alone. A refusal is `UnauthorizedAssociationOrchestrationException`, with the leaves' messages.
   3. **The suggestion's fields**, in one validation (TagSuggestions.md rule 2): `ContentItemId` is not empty, *"Id is required"*; and `Name`, once trimmed of white space at both ends, is not empty, *"Text is required"*, and is at most 30 characters, *"Text exceed max length of 30 characters"*. A refusal is `InvalidAssociationOrchestrationException`, *"Tag suggestion is invalid, fix the errors and try again."*, naming `ContentItemId` or `Name` in its `Data`. Nothing strips a hash, and nothing changes letter case. Every step below reads the trimmed name.
   4. **The post, resolved as the upsert resolves an endpoint**: through `ResolveEndpointAsync` (`AssociationOrchestrationService.Endpoints.cs`), for a `ContentItem` endpoint keyed on `ContentItemId`, under the caller's envelope, so it reads `IContentItemService.RetrieveContentItemByIdAsync` as the ambient caller of this direct call (§DOM4.5). It is handed the endpoint name `A`, which resolution puts in its refusal, as the upsert hands it for its endpoint A (`AssociationOrchestrationService.cs`), since the post is endpoint A (step 5). A post that does not exist, or that the caller may not see, is refused as resolution refuses one, `NotFoundAssociationOrchestrationException`, *"The A endpoint was not found."* (§SEC14.5 rule 1): the upsert's refusal of the same post, to which rule 8 holds this member.
   5. **The pair.** The post is endpoint A, carrying the group id, content type and scope resolution derived for it (§DOM4.5), and the tag is endpoint B, of type `Tag`, its key id and group id the tag's id once step 9 or 10 knows it, and its scope `EntityTypeVersioning.DefaultScopeFor(EntityType.Tag)`, as `DeriveEndpoint` derives a tag endpoint. `UserId` is `null`: the pair is editorial (§DOM4.10 rule 2). That order is canonical (§DOM4.4), and the foundation restores canonical order itself in any case (§DOM4.4 rule 4).
   6. **The pairing's endpoint veto**, over the pair, as `ValidateUserIsNotBlockedFromEndpoints` asks it of the upsert's editorial pair: `ContentItem-ReadOnly`, `ContentItem-{ContentType}-ReadOnly` for the post's derived type, and `Tag-ReadOnly` (§SEC14.7 posture A′ rule 1). It covers the tag's add's own veto, `ReadOnly` or `Tag-ReadOnly` (TagSuggestions.md rule 9, step 4). A refusal is `UnauthorizedAssociationOrchestrationException`.
   7. **A rejected post.** Where the post's `ApprovalStatus`, read by step 4 for the version `ContentItemId` names, is `Rejected`, the suggestion is refused with the write flow's own refusal of a rejected endpoint, asked of endpoint A: `InvalidAssociationOrchestrationException`, *"The A endpoint is rejected, so it cannot be paired."* (RejectedEndpoints.md rules 1 and 10; `AssociationOrchestrationService.RejectedEndpoints.md §1` rule 3). It is #1358's refusal, written once, never a second copy (*Deviations* 1), and rule 8 holds its answer to the upsert's. #1358 writes it as one step inside `DeriveAssociationToAddAsync`, adding no member (`AssociationOrchestrationService.RejectedEndpoints.md §1` rules 1 and 9). This member's build draws that step out into a member of its own, which the write flow asks of A and of B as before and this member asks of A, so the step moves and is not copied, and #1358's tests stand unchanged. It reads the status `ResolvedEndpoint` keeps for an endpoint once #1358 has it kept (`AssociationOrchestrationService.RejectedEndpoints.md §1` rule 2), and reads nothing again.
   8. **The facet gate**, for the post as host and a `Tag` far end, as the upsert asks it (`ValidateSettingsAllowTheFacetAsync`, `AssociationOrchestrationService.FacetGate.cs`; §ARC16.2.1): the post's winning setting must carry `TagsAllowed`, and a post whose setting resolves no row is refused too. Each refusal is `InvalidAssociationOrchestrationException`, naming `TagsAllowed`, as the gate names it.
   9. **The read by name**: `ITagService.FindTagByNameAsync` with the trimmed name (`Backend/Foundations/TagService.TagSuggestions.md §3`). Where it answers a match whose `ApprovalStatus` is `Rejected`, deleted or not, or a match that is soft-deleted, the tag is **withheld**: no tag is added, no pairing is inserted, and the member goes on to steps 11 and 12 with the withheld tag's id on endpoint B, so that its answer is rule 2's and tells it from no other tag the caller cannot see (TagSuggestions.md rule 4; RejectedEndpoints.md rule 12; §SEC14.5 rules 1–3). Before the answer is returned, the true reason is logged as information, naming the tag's id and whether it is rejected or soft-deleted, and noting that the tag was not paired and the caller was not told so (§SEC14.5 rules 6 and 7). Nothing is thrown for it. Any other match is the tag the suggestion pairs, whatever its status and whoever owns it (TagSuggestions.md rules 4 and 5).
   10. **The add of a new tag**, where the read answers no match: `ITagService.AddTagAsync` with a new `Tag` carrying the trimmed name and `ApprovalStatus` `Submitted`, and nothing else (TagSuggestions.md rule 14). The add mints the tag's `Id` (`Backend/Foundations/TagService.TagSuggestions.md §1`), and stamps its audit fields under the caller, so the tag is the caller's. Where the add is refused as already holding the name — `TagDependencyValidationException` whose inner exception is `AlreadyExistsTagException` — the member reads the name once more, through step 9, and goes on with the match it finds as step 9 has it, a rejected or soft-deleted one withheld; where that second read finds no match, the add's refusal is thrown on (TagSuggestions.md rule 10). It never adds twice.
   11. **Whether the caller can see the tag** (TagSuggestions.md rule 5), for a tag step 9 found, or step 10's second read: `ITagService.RetrieveTagByIdAsync` with the tag's id, under the caller's envelope — the ambient caller of this direct call, as endpoint resolution reads a tag endpoint (`ResolveEndpointCoreAsync`, `AssociationOrchestrationService.Endpoints.cs`). Where it answers the tag, the caller can see it. Where it refuses it as not found — `TagValidationException` whose inner exception is `NotFoundTagException`, which the tag's read answers for a row the caller may not see and for every soft-deleted row (§SEC14.7 posture A rule 4; §SEC14.5 rules 1 and 3) — the caller cannot see it. That refusal is a step of the flow and refuses nothing: it decides which answer rule 2 gives, never whether the tag is paired, which steps 9 and 12 decide over reads no identity filters. Any other exception it raises reaches the arms rule 4 names. A tag step 10 added is the caller's own, and is not read again.
   12. **The occupancy check**, the editorial arm's, over the pair with the tag's id on endpoint B, a withheld tag's included: `FindPairOccupantsAsync` with no read envelope (`AssociationOrchestrationService.cs`), the exact-pair probe and then, where it finds no row, the overlap probe (TagSuggestions.md rule 6).
   13. **The insert of a free pair**, where neither probe finds a row and the tag is not withheld, whether or not the caller can see it: `IAssociationService.AddAssociationAsync` with the pair at `Submitted`, as the editorial arm hands its working copy to the add (`AssociationOrchestrationService.md §6` rule 3). The add mints the row's `Id`. Where the add refuses the pair as already held — `AssociationDependencyValidationException` whose inner exception is `AlreadyExistsAssociationException`, another reader's insert of the same pair having landed between step 12's probes and this insert — and step 11 found that the caller cannot see the tag, the refusal is not thrown on, and the answer is `Created`, as rule 2 answers every tag the caller cannot see (TagSuggestions.md rule 10, *Two readers pairing one tag at once*). It is a step of the flow, as step 10's second read is, and no catch arm. Where the caller can see the tag, the refusal is thrown on, as rule 4 has it.
2. **The answer** (TagSuggestions.md rule 7). Whose a probe's row is, is its `CreatedBy` against the caller's id, which is the envelope's `SecurityContext.SubjectId`, the id the security-audit broker stamps as `CreatedBy` (`SecurityContextPrincipalFactory.cs`), compared ordinally, as the foundations compare `CreatedBy` in their owner tests. Either probe's row counts alike: the exact-pair probe's, or, where that finds none, the overlap probe's, which is always live.
   - **`Created`** where step 13 inserted the pair, whether the tag was found or added; wherever step 11 found that the caller cannot see the tag, whatever the probes answered, a withheld tag's included (TagSuggestions.md rule 5, *A withheld tag suggested twice*), and whatever step 13's insert answered, its refusal of a pair already held included (TagSuggestions.md rule 10, *Two readers pairing one tag at once*); and where the tag is withheld, the caller can see it, and neither probe answered a row, though nothing was inserted (TagSuggestions.md rule 4, *A withheld tag's code*).
   - **`AlreadySuggestedByCaller`** where the caller can see the tag and a probe answered a live row at `Draft` or `Submitted` that the caller created (*The reader's own pending pairing*), a row the overlap probe found after a publisher re-scoped it included.
   - **`PreviouslySuggestedByCaller`** where the caller can see the tag and a probe answered a row the caller created that is not pending: live at `Approved` or `Rejected`, or soft-deleted at any status (*The caller's own pairing that is no longer pending*).
   - **`AlreadySuggested`** where the caller can see the tag and a probe answered a row another caller created, at any status, live or soft-deleted (*A pairing that was itself rejected*).

   It carries the status alone. Nothing is written where the pair is occupied, nor for a withheld tag. A tag the caller cannot see is still paired where its pair is free and it is not withheld (step 13).
3. **It writes nothing else, and publishes nothing of its own.** Its writes are the tag's add and the pairing's add, each publishing its own fact, `Tag-Added` and `Association-Added` (TagSuggestions.md rule 13). It writes nothing to a tag it finds.
4. **No catch arm is added.** Every member shares one `TryCatch` (`AssociationOrchestrationService.md §4` rule 9). Its own refusals are this service's validation family, through the arms it has: `NullAssociationOrchestrationException`, `UnauthorizedAssociationOrchestrationException`, `NotFoundAssociationOrchestrationException` and `InvalidAssociationOrchestrationException`, each mapped to `AssociationOrchestrationValidationException` and logged as an error (`CreateAndLogValidationExceptionAsync`). A refusal from `IAssociationService` is its dependency validation exception, and a failure its dependency exception, as for the upsert, save the one refusal step 13 answers: the insert's refusal of a pair already held, for a tag the caller cannot see. For a tag the caller can see, that refusal is the dependency validation exception too, which the route answers `409`. Anything `ITagService` or `IContentItemService` raises past the steps above, step 11's read included save the not-found that step answers, and the facet gate's failed settings read, reach the arms they reach for the upsert: the closing `catch (Xeption)` as this service's dependency exception, and the closing `catch (Exception)` as its service exception (TagSuggestions.md rule 11).
5. **It takes a `CancellationToken`**, so a cancelled token is asked first, before the null check, as the upsert asks it, and a cancelled token and a timeout are answered through the `TryCatch`'s two cancellation arms (`AssociationOrchestrationService.Exceptions.cs`).
6. **A soft-deleted tag is withheld by step 9 until #1362's undelete replaces that on this route** (TagSuggestions.md rule 4; §ARC12.3.1 rule 5). #1362's design adds the section that does it, beside this one, and a soft-deleted tag that is also `Rejected` stays withheld.
7. **The code text it adds, or makes untrue, is written with it.** The interface's summary of the member (`IAssociationOrchestrationService.cs`) says what it finds, adds and pairs, that a tag the caller cannot see is paired, that a tag the caller cannot see is answered `Created` whatever holds the pair, that a rejected or soft-deleted tag is withheld, what it refuses, and that it answers a status and no id. The class's summary (`AssociationOrchestrationService.cs`), which lists what the service does, gains the suggestion. The member's gate composition carries a comment naming it as the suggestion's, as its neighbours under *One composition per operation, over shared leaves* name theirs. And the comments on the members it shares with the upsert say it shares them, where they say they are the upsert's or its two doors' alone: above `ValidateUserIsNotBlockedFromEndpoints` (`AssociationOrchestrationService.Validations.cs`), *"THIS IS THE UPSERT'S ALONE"*; above `FindPairOccupantsAsync` (`AssociationOrchestrationService.cs`), *"the second half of the add's write flow and shared by both entry paths"*; above `ValidateSettingAllowsTheFacet` (`AssociationOrchestrationService.FacetGate.cs`), *"resolution has already shown the caller both endpoints"*, which for the suggestion is the host alone, the far end being a type before any tag is read; and the member rule 1's step 7 draws #1358's refusal of a rejected endpoint out into carries a comment saying the write flow and the suggestion share it, as the comments above their other shared members say.
8. **The write flow's refusals hold here too** (*Deviations* 1: the rules that decide a pairing are written once). The member reaches them through the members the write flow uses, and a theory holds it to them. Each refusal the write-flow seam's `WriteFlowRefusals` list names (`AssociationOrchestrationServiceTests.Substrate.WriteFlow.cs`) is driven through `UpsertAssociationAsync`, for the post paired with a tag the caller may see, and through this member, for the same post, and both must answer it alike. Where an entry is about an endpoint, it is driven on the post, endpoint A on both: *"an endpoint that does not resolve"* is the post's read refusing it, and *"a caller blocked from an endpoint's content type"* a block on the post's type. An entry a suggestion cannot meet is excepted by name, with its reason: today *"a missing endpoint key"*, since the suggestion is handed no tag's key and its own fields are step 3's. An entry the theory neither drives nor excepts fails it. So a rule added to the write flow later, which the list's comment has join the list, reaches this member or is excepted in the open, and a refusal worded differently on the two fails. The entry #1359 adds, *"a rejected endpoint"* (`AssociationOrchestrationService.RejectedEndpoints.md §2` rule 5), is driven with the post rejected, and the theory carries its case whichever of #1359 and this member lands first. The list's comment says it drives this member too. The facet gate stays outside the list, as its comment has it, and step 8 asks it through the same member as the upsert.

## Deviations

1. **The dependency count** — §ARC12.5, the two-to-three rule for an orchestration. This service holds seven entity services: `IAssociationService`, `IContentItemService`, `ITagService`, `IReactionService`, `IBibleReferenceService`, `ICommentService` and `ILinkService`. §1 adds one operation to it, and no dependency: it calls three of the seven. **Why:** the suggestion pairs a tag with a post, and the rules that decide a pairing — the post's resolution, the endpoint veto, the refusal of a rejected post, the facet gate and the occupancy check — are this service's, written once for the two doors that add a pairing (§ARC12.5.2 rule 6: *"Because the two doors share one write flow, a rule added to it later is on both by construction."*). A suggestion reaching them anywhere else would be a second copy of each. It reaches them here, through the members that hold them, and §1 rule 8 holds it to the write flow's refusals. **What was rejected:**
   - **A service of its own**, holding `IContentItemService`, `ITagService` and `IAssociationService` — three, within two-to-three. It would copy every one of those rules and the endpoint resolution beneath them, and leave two copies to drift. And `AssociationsController` binds one service (§EVN13 rule 3; `the-standard-exposers` ts-exposers-001), so the suggestion's route would need a controller of its own, where the owner asked for the association controller to be extended (TagSuggestions.md, *Where the submission lives*).
   - **A processing service for the find-or-add, beneath this one.** A read by name followed by an add, over one entity, is a probe-then-write, which earns a processing service (§ARC12.1 rules 1 and 3). But this service reaches its entities through their foundation services, and an orchestration holds one kind of service, never a mix (§ARC12.5; `.claude/agents/planner.md`, *One kind of dependency, never a mix*). A tag processing service beside the six foundation services would mix them, and reaching every entity through a processing service would put a processing service over six entities none of whose flows earns one.
   - **The find-or-add in the tag foundation**, as one member. Higher-order logic composing an entity's foundation calls is processing work, not the foundation's (§ARC12.1 rule 1), and the member would answer a hidden row's id on the contract `TagsController` binds (§EVN13 rule 3; §SEC14.5 rules 1 and 3).

   The Likes user story's deviation (`AssociationOrchestrationService.md`, *Deviations* 1) names these seven for its own operations and is not this deviation's reason; nor is any other service's. §ARC12.5 entry 1's provisional note, which leaves open whether a processing service is earned beneath this service for the retrieve-or-add write, is unchanged. **Approved** by the owner on 2026-10-26 (TagSuggestions.md, *Both deviations are signed off*: *"The planner records both as approved by you, with today's date."*).
