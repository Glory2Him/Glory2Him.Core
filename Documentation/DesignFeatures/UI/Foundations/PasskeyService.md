# Passkey service (React)
Parent: [BrokersHoldNoLogic.md](../../BrokersHoldNoLogic.md)
Level: foundation service — `passkeyService` (`Websites/Glory2Him.WebApp.React/src/services/foundations/passkeyService.ts`), existing, with its WebAuthn ceremony (`src/hooks/usePasskeys.ts`, *Where the ceremony stands*)
Inherits: §UI20.9.2 departure 3, §UI20.9.3 rules 4 and 5, `UI/Brokers/PasskeyBroker.md`, `the-standard-reacttypescript-services`, `the-standard-reacttypescript-brokers` and `the-standard-reacttypescript-files` as *Deviations* departs from them

The React hooks over `PasskeyBroker` and the browser's WebAuthn ceremony. `usePasskeySignIn` runs the sign-in ceremony through `requestPasskeyAndSignInAsync` in `src/hooks/usePasskeys.ts`, which asks the broker for the request options. Under §UI20.9.3 rule 5 this hook decides the username the ceremony is handed (§1), and the ceremony hands on what it is given. How this hook and `useAddPasskey` depart from the services skill, and the departures the ceremony's placement decides for it, are recorded under *Deviations*.

## Where the ceremony stands

**`src/hooks/usePasskeys.ts` is part of `passkeyService`, at the foundation level (§UI20.3), and stays where it is.** It is the WebAuthn ceremony that `useAddPasskey` and `usePasskeySignIn` run, and nothing else runs it: its two ceremony functions, `createAndRegisterPasskeyAsync` and `requestPasskeyAndSignInAsync`, are called by those two hooks alone (`passkeyService.ts`), and the pages import nothing from it but its error type, `PasskeyCeremonyError` (`passkeys.tsx`, `passkeySignInButton.tsx`). This document is its user story document, and the departures its placement decides are recorded under *Deviations*, with the two hooks'.

1. **It is not a hook**, though it stands under `src/hooks/` with a hook's name. It exports its two ceremony functions, `browserSupportsPasskeys` and `PasskeyCeremonyError`, and no hook, and it calls no React hook.
2. **It is not part of `PasskeyBroker`.** Each ceremony function makes two requests of the server for one call, and decides what the browser's failures mean. The first is logic by §UI20.9.3 rule 1's test, and the second as §UI20.9.3 rule 7 counts deciding what a failure means, and a broker holds none (`BrokersHoldNoLogic.md` rule 1).
3. **What it does is a foundation service's work.** It calls one broker, `PasskeyBroker`, on either side of the browser's ceremony, and turns the browser's failures into an error of its own. So `passkeyService` wraps one broker, `PasskeyBroker`, through its hooks and its ceremony alike. The browser's authenticator, which the ceremony reaches without a broker, is *Deviations* 4.
4. **It keeps §UI20.9.3 rule 5.** Being part of `passkeyService` puts nothing between `usePasskeySignIn` and `PasskeyBroker` that §1 does not design. How the hook treats the email it is handed is §1's, and how the ceremony treats the username it is handed, and the member of `PasskeyBroker` it calls with it, are §1 rule 3's.
5. **It calls for no change.** It keeps its file, its name and what it holds as they are. What that placement departs from, in what it calls, what it constructs, and its folder and name, is *Deviations* 4 to 6.

## 1. usePasskeySignIn (#957)

```ts
usePasskeySignIn: () => UseMutationResult<CurrentUser, unknown, string>
```

1. **It asks for the request options for the email the reader typed, trimmed.**
2. **An email that is blank once trimmed is no username**, and it hands none on, so the request carries no `username`, as today when the reader typed nothing.
3. **The ceremony hands the username it is given to `PostRequestOptionsAsync` unchanged** (`UI/Brokers/PasskeyBroker.md §1`). `requestPasskeyAndSignInAsync` takes `username: string | undefined` in place of the email, and neither trims nor tests it (§UI20.9.3 rule 5). The rest of the ceremony, and its errors, are unchanged.
4. **What it writes to the cache on success is unchanged.**

## Deviations

§UI20.9.2's departures do not reach `useAddPasskey` and `usePasskeySignIn` (*Outside this section*). They construct no broker: they run the browser's WebAuthn ceremony through `src/hooks/usePasskeys.ts`, which calls `PasskeyBroker` for them and raises its own error type, `PasskeyCeremonyError`, which their callers branch on (`passkeys.tsx`, `passkeySignInButton.tsx`). The ceremony is part of this service (*Where the ceremony stands*), so its departures are recorded here too. Departures 1 to 3 are the two hooks' and the ceremony's, from `the-standard-reacttypescript-services`. Departures 4 to 6 are the ceremony's, from the brokers, services and files skills. What `passkeyService` is as a whole, its file, its name and its shape as an object of hooks, is §UI20.9.2 departure 3's, as every foundation service's is. **This section is awaiting the owner's approval.**

1. **tsr-services-002 — *"Foundation services MUST validate all inputs before calling brokers."* — tsr-services-011 — *"Services MUST validate for null/undefined inputs."* — and tsr-services-014 — *"Services SHOULD validate structural integrity (e.g., valid email format, date ranges)."*** Neither hook, nor the ceremony, validates what it is handed or throws a validation error. How `usePasskeySignIn` treats the email it is handed is §1's, and how the ceremony treats the username it is handed is §1 rule 3's (§UI20.9.3 rule 5). **Why:** nothing these hooks are handed can be invalid to the server. `useAddPasskey` is handed nothing. The server answers a request for sign-in options with a username or without one, and looks a username up only when it is not empty, by name (`FindByNameAsync`), not as an email (`PasskeyApiEndpoints.cs`, `/request-options`). It signs in the account it finds from the passkey's own credential, not from what the reader typed (`/login`). So a check in the hook, an email's format above all, would refuse a sign-in the server accepts. **Instead:** the hooks and the ceremony validate nothing they are handed, and the server answers what it is sent.
2. **tsr-services-003 — *"Foundation services MUST map broker exceptions to service-layer exceptions (ValidationException, DependencyException, ServiceException)."* — tsr-services-004 — *"Service methods MUST use try-catch pattern when calling brokers."* — tsr-services-007 — *"Services MUST implement TryCatch wrapper for exception mapping and logging."* — tsr-services-010 — *"Services MUST define clear exception types: ValidationException, DependencyException, ServiceException."* — tsr-services-017 — *"Broker exceptions MUST be caught and wrapped in DependencyException."* — tsr-services-019 — *"Unexpected service errors MUST be wrapped in ServiceException."* — tsr-services-020 — *"All service exceptions MUST preserve the inner exception for debugging."* — and tsr-services-021 — *"Exception messages MUST be user-facing and actionable."*** The hooks catch nothing. The ceremony wraps no `PasskeyBroker` call, so a failed request rejects with its `AxiosError`, unchanged. It raises `PasskeyCeremonyError` when the browser lacks what passkeys need, when the authenticator's call fails, and when that call gives back no credential. Its message is one the ceremony wrote, or the failed call's own message where the ceremony has none for it, and it is empty when the reader cancelled the ceremony (an `AbortError`). It keeps no inner error. Parsing the server's options is outside its `try`, so a failure there rejects with the browser's own error. Nothing logs. **Why:** the two hooks fail in two ways that their pages tell apart and answer differently. A `PasskeyCeremonyError` carries a message that the page shows the reader, or none, when the reader cancelled, and then the page shows nothing. A failed request carries the server's answer, which the page reads off the `AxiosError`, falling back to a message of its own for any other error (`extractApiErrorMessage`, `statusMessage.tsx`). The skill's service exceptions would have to carry both the cancel and the server's answer through for the pages to do either, which is the same two errors under other names. **Instead:** the ceremony's failures reach the caller in three shapes. A browser that lacks what passkeys need, a failed call of the authenticator's to create or get a credential, and such a call that gives back no credential reach it as `PasskeyCeremonyError`. A failure to parse the server's options reaches it as the browser's own error, and `PasskeyBroker`'s failures as the `AxiosError` it raised. A caller tells `PasskeyCeremonyError` apart with `instanceof PasskeyCeremonyError`, as both pages do, and answers the other two as any other error. **Not approved here:** neither hook turns the app's global error toast off (`meta.suppressGlobalErrorToast`), so today the toast announces each of their failures a second time, beside the page's own message, and announces a cancelled ceremony, which the pages leave unannounced, as "An unknown error has occurred, please try again." (`apiBroker.globals.ts`). That is a defect this departure does not approve, and §2 and §3 remove it.
3. **tsr-services-006 — *"Service method names SHOULD mirror broker method names (e.g., broker: `getPatientAsync` → service: `retrievePatientByIdAsync`)."*** Neither hook, nor either of the ceremony's two functions, `createAndRegisterPasskeyAsync` and `requestPasskeyAndSignInAsync`, mirrors a `PasskeyBroker` member's name. **Why:** each hook runs one reader action through one ceremony function, and that function makes two `PasskeyBroker` calls, one on either side of the browser's ceremony. `useAddPasskey` runs `createAndRegisterPasskeyAsync`, which asks for the creation options and registers the credential (`GetCreationOptionsAsync`, `RegisterPasskeyAsync`). `usePasskeySignIn` runs `requestPasskeyAndSignInAsync`, which asks for the request options (§1 rule 3, §UI20.9.3 rule 5) and signs in (`PasskeyLoginAsync`). So no one member's name says what a hook or a ceremony function does. And React's rules of hooks, which the app's lint enforces (`eslint-plugin-react-hooks`, in `eslint.config.js`), require a hook's name to start with `use`. **Instead:** each hook is named `use` and the reader's action. Each ceremony function is named for the credential the browser's ceremony creates or requests and the call that follows it: `createAndRegisterPasskeyAsync` for the passkey it creates and registers, and `requestPasskeyAndSignInAsync` for the passkey it requests and signs in with. All four keep their names.
4. **tsr-brokers-001 — *"Brokers MUST be the only layer that talks to external dependencies (APIs, storage, logging, external services)."*** The ceremony calls the browser's authenticator itself. It calls `navigator.credentials.create` and `navigator.credentials.get`, and `PublicKeyCredential`'s static functions that parse the server's options, and it reads both to tell whether the browser supports passkeys (`browserSupportsPasskeys`). **Why:** a broker of its own for the authenticator would give `passkeyService` a second broker, against tsr-services-001 — *"Foundation services MUST wrap exactly one broker — no more, no less."* Short of that, it would take a second foundation service to wrap the new broker, and a layer above both services to compose them, for this one flow. As it stands, the service wraps `PasskeyBroker` alone. And the ceremony is a module of its own, which a test of either hook replaces with `vi.mock`, as a service's test replaces its broker's module (`contentItemService.test.tsx`), so such a test stands in for the authenticator by replacing that one module, as `passkeyService.test.tsx` does for `usePasskeySignIn`. **Instead:** the ceremony reaches the authenticator, and no other code in the app does. It reaches the server only through `PasskeyBroker`.
5. **tsr-brokers-009 — *"Brokers MUST be injected via dependency injection, not instantiated directly in services."* — and tsr-services-005 — *"Foundation services MUST use dependency injection to receive broker instances."*** Each ceremony function constructs the `PasskeyBroker` it calls (`new PasskeyBroker()`), outside `src/services/`. §UI20.9.1 departure 4 approves a foundation service hook that constructs its own broker, and does not reach a module of functions. **Why:** the ceremony is two functions, not a class, so it has no constructor to receive a broker through. A parameter would only move the construction into each hook, which would construct the broker just the same. A test reaches the ceremony's broker by replacing the broker's module with `vi.mock`, as `contentItemService.test.tsx` replaces `ContentItemBroker`'s, so the ceremony can be tested against a substitute broker, as injection would allow. **Instead:** each ceremony function constructs the `PasskeyBroker` it calls, and a test substitutes the broker's module.
6. **tsr-files-002 — *"File names MUST describe architectural role using standard patterns."* — tsr-files-007 — *"Foundation service files MUST follow pattern `{domain}Service.ts` (e.g., `patientService.ts`)."* — and tsr-services-022 — *"Foundation services MUST be in `services/foundations/{domain}/` directory."*** The ceremony is part of a foundation service, but stands in `src/hooks/usePasskeys.ts`, under a hook's folder and with a hook's name. It is neither in `src/services/foundations/` nor in a file named for its service, and so not where §UI20.9.2 departure 3 puts a foundation service either. **Why:** no place keeps it a module of its own and follows tsr-files-002. The skill has no pattern for part of a foundation service kept in a file of its own, and it stays a module of its own because that is what a test of the two hooks replaces (*Deviations* 4). Folding it into `passkeyService.ts` would follow tsr-files-007, but a test of either hook would then have to stand in for the authenticator and for `PasskeyBroker`'s two calls, not for one module. A move into `src/services/foundations/`, beside `passkeyService.ts`, would put it in the folder §UI20.9.2 departure 3 gives a foundation service, so it would depart from tsr-services-022 only as every foundation service does, and it would take it out of the hooks skill's reach. But it would follow neither tsr-files-002 nor tsr-files-007 there either, since the one file name that departure gives the service, `passkeyService.ts`, holds the hooks. And it would change its three importers, `passkeyService.ts`, `passkeys.tsx` and `passkeySignInButton.tsx`, and the test that mocks it by its path, `passkeyService.test.tsx`. Kept where it is, it changes no file. So the move would settle one of the three rules, and the hooks skill's reach, at the cost of four files, and leave the file-name departures standing; this record proposes it stays. **Instead:** it stays `src/hooks/usePasskeys.ts`, and this document says what it is (*Where the ceremony stands*). It is not a hook, so the hooks skill, whose `applies-to` reaches it by its path (`src/hooks/**/*.ts`), does not describe it, and none of that skill's rules for hooks, such as tsr-hooks-008, is departed from here.
