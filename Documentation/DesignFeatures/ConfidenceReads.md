# Confidence reads
Parent: [BereanReview.md](BereanReview.md)
Inherits: §SEC14.2 rules 1–3, §SEC14.3, §SEC14.5 rules 1 and 4, §SEC14.6 rules 1–4, §SEC14.6.1, §SEC14.7 posture A rules 2, 4, 7 and 8, posture A′ rules 2, 3, 5, 6 and 7, §SEC14.8 rule 1, §SEC18.6 rules 4 and 5, §ARC12.2.1, §ARC16.7.2, §APR8.6.1 rules 2 and 4, §APR8.6.2 (*The confidence suggestion* rules 6 and 7), BereanReview.md rules 1, 2, 7, 20 and 24, `Backend/Models/IApproval.md §1` rules 6 and 7, AssociationReads.md rule 8
Mockups: none. No page changes: a page renders nothing of a score it is answered as null (BereanReview.md I1.3).
Design task: #1230

An approvable item's confidence score, its reason and their provenance are read by the item's review tier alone, and every other caller is answered all four as null. §SEC14.7 posture A rule 8 is that rule. This sub-feature builds it on every operation that answers an item to a caller, and names each surface it leaves as it is, with the reason.

## Problem

When this design was written (2026-10-17), every read of an approvable item answered the item's four confidence fields to every caller its posture admits to the row: the item's owner, any signed-in reader of a published post, and the anonymous reader of `/api/contentItems/Public`. Only an association stored them then. The other seven items gain them through `IApproval` (`Backend/Models/IApproval.md §1`, #1154), and store them once their columns are mapped (§2 there, #1196). The owner ruled that only the item's review tier reads the score and the reason, and their provenance with them, and that everyone else is answered all four as null rather than refused (BereanReview.md R6 and R8, rule 24). No score is written until that holds (BereanReview.md, *What ships together* step 9).

## Business rules

1. **The rule is §SEC14.7 posture A rule 8**, which this document builds and does not restate. The rules below say where it reaches, what it leaves, and how each part is built.

2. **The review tier is the one the read's own admit asks, of the same row.** Each foundation already composes a row's review tier for posture A rule 4's read admit, and the withholding asks that same question of that same row, so a read that admits the review tier to a non-public row and the read that answers it the four never disagree about who the tier is:
   - **`ContentItem`**: the global `Reviewers`, `Publishers` and `Administrators`, `ContentItem-Reviewers` and `ContentItem-Publishers`, and the `ContentItem-%ContentType%-Reviewers` and `-Publishers` of the row's own `ContentType` (§SEC18.6 rules 4 and 5). It is `HasReviewRole` in `ContentItemService.Validations.cs`, and on a collection `HasBroadReviewRole` and `ReviewableContentTypes`.
   - **`Link`, `Tag`, `Reaction`, `Comment` and `BibleReference`**: the global three and the entity's own `-Reviewers` and `-Publishers`. Each is its service's `HasReviewRole`, which takes no row: a caller holds it for every row of the entity or for none.
   - **`Association`**: the global three, or a coarse or narrow review or publisher role matching either endpoint, the endpoint's type tested beside its content type (§SEC14.7 posture A′ rules 2, 5 and 6). It is `HasReviewRoleForAssociation` in `AssociationService.Validations.cs`, and on a collection the global check and the sets `ResolveReviewableEntityTypes` and `ResolveReviewableContentTypes` compose in `AssociationService.cs`.

   It is the tier the confidence suggestion answers (§APR8.6.2, *The confidence suggestion* rule 6), which BereanReview.md rule 24 names. No read-only role is asked (posture A′ rule 3). The owner — `CreatedBy`, or on a personal association `UserId` (posture A′ rule 7) — is admitted to the row and not to the four, unless they hold the tier. A caller with no security context, an anonymous caller and the system identity hold no role, so each is answered the four as null.

3. **Where it reaches: every operation that answers an item's row to a caller.** Each row names the operation, what reaches it, what it answers today, and the section that builds the rule on it. *Today* is `main` at this design: an association's four are stored, and the other seven items carry none until #1154 gives them the properties, null on every read until #1196 maps the columns, and stored from then. *The tier* is rule 2's. Each section covers the operation's event door where it has one, because the change is in the body the door shares with the direct path, as `Backend/Foundations/ContentItemService.BereanReview.md §1–§2` cover theirs.

   **Every read above the foundation composes over these**, and takes the rule from them with no change of its own: the processing tiers' reads by id, their collection, group, latest and published reads, their feed, their modify and remove, and their doors; and the association orchestration's two reads and its writes. The processing tiers' two public collection reads are rule 7's, and are in the table.

4. **What it leaves as it is, and why.**
   - **An add.** The row it answers holds no score: each item's add refuses the four (`Backend/Foundations/<Entity>Service.BereanReview.md §1`; `Backend/Foundations/AssociationService.md §15`), and the processing tier's duplicate acknowledgement composes a row with none (§DOM3.4.2 rule 6). Until an item's refusal is built, its add answers back only what its caller sent.
   - **An operation whose every caller is the review tier or the system**: each item's approval transition (the publisher tier, which the review tier contains, or the system identity), unpublish (`Administrators` or the system), hard remove (`Administrators`), set-confidence (the system alone, BereanReview.md rule 1) and the association's set-scope (`Publishers` or `Administrators`). Everyone each answers may read the four, so each answers them as stored (§SEC14.7 posture A rule 8).
   - **`IAssociationService.UpsertPersonalAssociationAsync`.** Its one caller, the orchestration's upsert, answers its caller a status and an id (`AssociationSuggestionResult`), never the row.
   - **Reads that answer no row**: the duplicate-content probe, the higher-version and highest-version probes, the published-sibling probe, `RetrievePublicContentItemGroupsAsync` and `FindPublicContentItemGroupAsync` (an id, a group and a content type), the association's pair, overlap and personal lookups, its reaction counts and the caller's own reactions.
   - **`IReactionService.RetrievePublicReactionsAsync`.** Its one caller, the reaction summary, answers counts and each reaction's name and emoji (§ARC16.8), never the row.
   - **The system's own reads**: `IAccessBroker`'s traversal of the item behind a round (`ResolveEntityAsync`, and `RetrieveApprovalSubjectConfidenceAsync`, `Backend/Brokers/AccessBroker.BereanReview.md §3`), each transition's read of the stored score for the decision (`Backend/Foundations/<Entity>Service.BereanReview.md §5`), set-confidence's read of the row it writes, and the review process's reads (#1216). Each reads unfiltered because it decides an invariant (BereanReview.md rule 20), and none answers a caller.
   - **Facts.** A fact is heard by the system's own subscribers, and no fact's row reaches a reader (§SEC14.8 rule 1). A foundation publishes its fact with the row as stored, and withholds from its answer alone (rule 8).
   - **A live update** carries no row (§SEC14.8 rule 1).
   - **Berean's status read** (`GET api/AIReviewers/…`) carries the confidence suggestion, which it already answers to the review tier alone (§APR8.6.2, *The confidence suggestion* rule 6), and no score.
   - **The verdict** (`GET api/Approvals/…/Verdict`) carries no field. Once the low-score block's gathers ship (BereanReview.md rule 10), its reasons can say that a score is below the rejection threshold or missing, and it answers the moderation tier alone (§ARC16.7.2). *Risks* records how that tier is matched today.
   - **Berean's vote and comments** are #1216's: what they say of the score is that design's (BereanReview.md rule 6).
   - **`Attachment`** has no service and no read, and its four stay null (BereanReview.md rule 2).

5. **Many items, each by its own tier.** A collection read and a group read answer each row by that row's tier. Where a caller is in the tier of some rows and not others — a narrow content-type reviewer among content items, or a scoped endpoint reviewer among associations — they are answered the first by their stored values and the rest as null. The association orchestration's reads compose over the foundation's withheld rows and add no term on the four. They resolve each endpoint through that endpoint's own foundation read only to decide whether it is visible (§SEC14.3), and answer associations alone, never an endpoint's row, so each association is answered by its own endpoints' tier.

6. **The query options read what the caller reads.** The routes with `[EnableQuery]` compose over a queryable their service hands up unenumerated: `GET api/ContentItems`, `GET api/ContentItems/Public`, `GET api/Links`, `GET api/Links/Public`, `GET api/Tags`, `GET api/Reactions`, `GET api/Comments`, `GET api/BibleReferences`, and, once built, `GET api/associations` (#1124). The host allows `$select`, `$filter`, `$orderby` and `$count` (`Websites/Glory2Him.WebApp/Program.cs`, the call to `AddOData`). The four are withheld inside the query (§SEC14.7 posture A rule 8), so each option composes over the withheld value:
   - **a `$filter`** naming one of the four tests the withheld value: a caller outside a row's tier is answered that row by `confidenceScore eq null`, and never by `confidenceScore gt 0` or `modelVersion ne null`, whatever is stored;
   - **an `$orderby`** on one of the four orders those rows as nulls, as it orders any null;
   - **a `$select`** naming one of the four answers its withheld value;
   - **`$count`** counts the rows the filter answered.

   So a caller outside the tier is answered the same rows, in the same order and with the same values, whether or not a score is stored, and a caller in the tier of some rows is answered those rows by their stored values. The group routes allow `$top`, `$skip` and `$count` alone (`ContentItemsController.GetContentItemsByGroupId`, `LinksController.GetLinksByGroupId`), compare nothing, and serve a group read that has already withheld. **Before #1196 maps the seven tables' columns**, a `$filter` or an `$orderby` naming one of the seven items' four fails, as `Backend/Models/IApproval.md §1` rule 6 records, and a query that names one of them cannot be translated either, so the six items' collection reads and the two public reads withhold inside their queries only once the columns are mapped (*What ships together* step 2). An association's columns are mapped already.

7. **A read that consults no security context answers all four as null to every caller.** There are three: the feed, `IContentItemService.RetrieveContentItemFeedAsync`, which mints no envelope (`ContentItemService.Lookup.cs`); and the two public collections, `IContentItemProcessingService.RetrieveAllPublicContentItemsAsync` and `ILinkProcessingService.RetrieveAllPublicLinksAsync`, which run their visibility filter with no security context (§SEC14.2 rule 1). Each answers an administrator what it answers an anonymous visitor (§SEC14.2 rule 1), so whoever calls it is answered as BereanReview.md rule 24's *anonymous reader of a published post* is. **The two public collections withhold all four themselves**, because each composes over its foundation's caller-widening collection read, which answers the caller's own tier (rule 3), and only the processing tier knows that its answer must not widen with the caller. §SEC14.2 rule 3's public read by id, designed and not yet built, is a read of the same footing, and answers all four as null when it is built (§SEC14.7 posture A rule 8). A reviewer reads the four through the caller-widening reads, which the admin area reads (§SEC14.2 rule 2).

8. **An operation that answers the row it wrote answers it by the same rule.** The general modify, the remove by id, the submit and an association's sort each answer their caller the row, and each is reached by a caller who may be outside the tier: the owner. Each answers the four by rule 2's tier, as stored where the caller holds it and null where they do not, on the direct path and in its door's reply alike. Each writes the row and publishes its fact as it does today, with the four as stored, and withholds them from its answer alone, as its last step.

9. **Withholding writes nothing, and nothing decides on what it withholds.** A withheld answer is a copy: the stored row keeps its four, and no storage write follows a read. A caller who sends a withheld row back clears nothing and is not refused, because the general modify takes the four from the stored row and never compares them (§SEC14.7 posture A rule 7; `Backend/Foundations/<Entity>Service.BereanReview.md §2`, `Backend/Foundations/AssociationService.BereanReview.md §3`). The processing tier's modify reads the row through the foundation's caller-facing read before it maps the caller's fields onto it (`ContentItemProcessingService.cs`, `LinkProcessingService.cs`), so the copy it hands the foundation's modify can be withheld, and the foundation takes the four from storage. Every flow that needs the stored score reads it unfiltered through `IAccessBroker` (BereanReview.md rule 20): a null a caller-facing read answers is never evidence that no score is stored.

10. **The review tier's pages go on reading the score.** The association moderation page reads the association by its id (`GET api/associations/{id}`, #1125), and its confidence block renders where both the score and the reason are answered (`PostModeration.md` rule 13; `Design/UI/Components/AssociationDetailPanel.md` rule 2.4). A caller in the association's tier is answered both and sees the block; a caller outside it is answered both as null and sees no block, as I1.3 has a null render nothing, and no suggestion, which Berean's status read answers to the tier alone. The post moderation page reads the item through `GET api/ContentItems/{contentItemId}` and shows no score (`Design/UI/Pages/ContentItemModerationDetailPage.md §6 item 24`); where that gap is built, it shows what that read answers. No page, component or hook changes.

11. **What changes for an association.** Until §1–§5 of `AssociationService.ConfidenceReads.md` are built, an association's four reach every caller its read admits to the row (§SEC14.7 posture A rule 4, posture A′ rule 7): through the foundation's two reads, which reach a caller through the orchestration's reads, no route serving them yet (#1124, #1125); through `Association-RetrievingById`'s reply; and through the answers of its modify, its remove by id and its sort. Once they are built, the association's review tier reads them, and every other caller who may read the association — its owner, a reader of a published pairing, an anonymous caller — is answered all four as null. **No page reads them yet**: the association page's confidence block is built under #1111 and its reads under #1125 (rule 10). **The two routes still answer every column** (AssociationReads.md rule 8): the four are answered null, not projected away. **No association payload is pinned**: the one pinned shape, `BranchPointContentItemProperties` (`Websites/Glory2Him.WebApp.Tests.Acceptance/Apis/ContentItems/ContentItemTests.PayloadShape.cs`), is a content item's, and keeps the four once #1154 adds them, since a withheld field stays in the shape. **What changes in the tests** is the unit tests whose caller is outside the tier: `CreateAssociationFiller` draws the four for every test but an add's (`UnsetOperationOwnedFields`), so a read test that returns a drawn row to such a caller and expects it as stored expects the four as null. Each section names its tests.

## Layer placement

1. **Each item's foundation decides and applies the rule**, on every operation of rule 3 it owns. The decision needs the caller's roles, which the operation's envelope carries (§SEC14.6 rule 4), and the row's own columns, which the foundation has just read; it is a row-level read rule, the foundation's altitude (§SEC14.6 rule 3), asked where rule 4's read admit is already asked of the same row. Applying it there reaches every caller, whichever layer they come through, since no read above the foundation answers a row the foundation did not answer (§SEC14.6 rule 1). One entity per service, no new dependency, and no layer above Foundation for any operation but rule 7's two.
2. **It is not a policy question for `IAccessClient`.** `IAccessClient` decides what needs rows a foundation may not read — the round's policy, its reviews and its comments (§APR8.6.1 rules 2 and 4). The tier needs no such row. Asking it there would give one tier two homes inside one read — the admit row-local, the withholding in the client — where §SEC14.3 keeps posture A′ rule 5's role resolution in the foundation's filter rather than re-deriving it above. And a collection read must withhold inside the query it hands up (rule 6), which a decision taken per row in a call cannot do. The confidence suggestion is decided in `IAccessClient` because it reads the round's thresholds, policy rows (§APR8.6.2, *The confidence suggestion* rule 7); the tier it asks is this one.
3. **Two reads sit at processing**, `RetrieveAllPublicContentItemsAsync` and `RetrieveAllPublicLinksAsync` (rule 7), because each is the processing tier's own caller-independent read composed over a caller-widening foundation read. The foundation beneath answers the caller's tier, which is right for every other caller of that read; only the processing read knows its answer is the anonymous visitor's. Each processing service depends on its one foundation service, as today.
4. **No orchestration, controller, hub or page changes.** The association orchestration composes over the foundation's withheld rows (rule 5). The exposers stay thin, and withholding in one is refused (§SEC14.7 posture A rule 8; posture A′ rule 7, *How it is closed*).

## Entity count, events and storage

- **Entities.** One per service: each foundation reads its own entity, and each processing service its foundation's. No flow spans two entity types.
- **Events.** None new, and none changed. A door's reply is its operation's answer (rule 8), and every fact keeps its content (rule 4).
- **Storage.** None. The seven tables' columns are #1196's, and an association's exist. No migration, no seed consequence.

## What ships together, and in what order

Every merge to `main` deploys (§APR7.4 item 6). No stage needs a pull request that closes more than one task.

1. **The six items' sections build on #1154**, which gives the six models the four properties. Before #1196 maps the columns each reads them back null, so each answers what it answers today; the tests seed the four on the row the mocked broker returns.
2. **The six items' collection reads and the two public reads build on #1196 too** (rule 6): each withholds inside a query that names the four, and a query cannot name a property storage ignores. #1196 waits for the six items' adds and modifies (`Backend/Models/IApproval.md §2` rule 3), so those land first, as they must for rule 9.
3. **`ContentItemService.ConfidenceReads.md §1` (#1234) is the first collection read**, because it sets the pattern the other collection reads and the two public reads follow: withholding inside the query, by a tier that differs row by row, proven against SQL Server. Each of the others builds on it.
4. **The association's sections build on #1229**, which has its modify take the four from the stored row in place of comparing them (`Backend/Foundations/AssociationService.BereanReview.md §3`). Before it, a caller outside the tier who read a withheld association and sent it back on a modify would be refused for four values they never touched (rule 9). The association's collection read and read by id change the same bodies as #1121, #1122 and #1123 (`Backend/Foundations/AssociationService.AssociationReads.md §1–§3`), and whichever lands second rebases on the first; neither needs the other.
5. **`AssociationService.ConfidenceReads.md §1` (#1273), the association's collection read, lands last**, after every other task of this sub-feature, and records the rule as built (`Design/design.md`, *A gap that is built stays in its list*, point 5). It is the read whose build leaves no surface of rule 3 answering the four to a caller outside the tier, so the design text that says otherwise stops being true in its pull request and no earlier one.
6. **Every task here is on `main` before the review process writes its first score** (BereanReview.md, *What ships together* step 9). Until then no item but an association holds a score.

## Risks

- **Reversible throughout.** No schema, no event and no migration: taking a section out widens what its operation answers again.
- **A property added to an item later must be carried by the withholding.** Where a collection read withholds inside its query by projecting the row, a property the projection does not name reads back as its default on that read alone. Each collection read's tests compare every other property with the stored row's, over a filler that draws every property, so a property added without its projection fails them.
- **The processing tier's facts carry the caller's view of the four.** `ContentItemProcessing-Modified` and `-Removed`, and their `LinkProcessing-` twins, carry the row their foundation answered the caller (`ContentItemProcessingService.cs`, `LinkProcessingService.cs`), so for an owner outside the tier their four read null where the foundation's own fact carries them as stored. Nothing reads the four off a fact (BereanReview.md rule 20), and the data change compares content fields, of which the four are none (BereanReview.md rule 9; #1215).
- **The processing tier's doors reach the foundation's direct reads**, which mint their envelope from the ambient caller (`IContentItemService`, the summary over the identity-carrying overload; #456). There the foundation decides the four against the same identity it decides the row's visibility against, so the two never disagree, and the case is the overloads' rather than this rule's.
- **The verdict's tier is matched by suffix today.** §ARC16.7.2 answers the verdict to the moderation tier, matched by any `-Reviewers` or `-Publishers` suffix, so a scoped reviewer of another entity type reads a round's verdict, and once the low-score block's gathers ship (BereanReview.md rule 10), its reason says that the item's score is below the rejection threshold or missing. It carries no field. The ruling that the round's routes gate on the item behind the round (§SEC14.7 posture A rule 5; ruled 2026-09-27, #699, not yet built) narrows the verdict to that item's tier.
- **A read answers a caller in some rows' tier a mixed page.** A narrow reviewer's `$orderby` on the score orders the rows they review among nulls for the rest. It tells them nothing about the rest, and is the rule working (rule 6).
